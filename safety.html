Our Approach

E&I work — whether we're pulling wire on a new greenfield station, doing hot-work near energized panels, or troubleshooting a live process at an operating facility — carries real hazards. We treat every task with the same level of planning and discipline, regardless of how routine it looks on paper.

We do not accept pressure to skip steps when schedules are tight. A JSA takes ten minutes. An incident takes much longer to undo — if it can be undone at all. That perspective is built into how our team operates, and it doesn't change based on who the client is or how fast the project is moving.

Lockout / Tagout (LOTO)

  • Full written LOTO procedures before any work on energized systems
  • Individual locks — every technician carries their own, every time
  • Verification of zero-energy state before work begins
  • LOTO log maintained and signed off at task completion
  • Awareness training for techs working near but not on energized equipment

Hot Work

  • Hot work permit required before any cutting, grinding, or welding
  • Continuous gas monitoring during all hot work in classified areas
  • Fire watch assigned and maintained for the duration and cool-down period
  • Area inspected and signed off by site safety or supervisor before work starts
  • Coordination with facility operator on permit issuance and area isolation

Gas Detection

  • Personal 4-gas monitors required for all work in or near compression areas
  • Pre-entry bump test verified and logged at start of each shift
  • Calibration records maintained per manufacturer requirements
  • Clear alarm thresholds — work stops, area is evacuated, no exceptions
  • Coordination with facility for confined space and classified-area entry procedures

Fall Protection

  • Fall protection required at 4 feet or per site-specific threshold — whichever is lower
  • Harness inspection completed before each use
  • Tie-off points identified and verified before climbing begins
  • Aerial lifts — operator trained and operator checklist completed before use
  • No improvised ladders or platforms; only rated equipment used on the job

Tool & Equipment Safety

  • Insulated tools rated for electrical work used on all E&I tasks
  • Hand tool inspection at the start of each shift; damaged tools taken out of service
  • Power tools inspected and GFCI-protected in wet or classified environments
  • No modified or field-fabricated tools permitted on any project
  • PPE matched to task — cut-resistant gloves, safety glasses, arc-rated clothing where applicable

Job Safety Analysis (JSA)

  • JSA completed before starting any new task or when conditions change
  • JSA developed by the crew doing the work — not imposed top-down
  • Each step reviewed for hazards; controls identified and assigned before work begins
  • All crew members sign the JSA and have the right to stop work if conditions change
  • JSAs retained in project safety documentation file

Stop-Work Authority

Every Rogue technician has the authority — and the responsibility — to stop work if something doesn't look right. No explanation required, no pressure to push through. If the job can't be done safely, the job doesn't get done until it can be. That's not a policy we wrote for a website. It's how our team has operated since day one, and it applies equally on installation jobs, commissioning jobs, and at operating facilities where the clock is always running.
