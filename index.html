What We Review

Design Review Scope

  • P&ID Review

    Line-by-line review against project specifications, design basis, and applicable standards. Tagged instrument verification against datasheet registers.

    • Instrument tag verification against datasheet register and line list
    • Control valve fail position, drain/vent spec, and interlock source confirmation
    • Utility system consistency check across all affected sheets
    • Tie-in, battery-limit, and interface definition review
    • Comments categorized: Safety-Critical, Hold, Clarification, Minor

  • Loop Drawing Verification

    Completeness and accuracy check of loop drawings against P&IDs, wiring schedules, and panel layouts. Signal continuity traced end-to-end.

    • Signal type confirmation: 4–20mA, digital I/O, HART, and data highway
    • Barrier and isolator matching for IS and Class I Div 2 field devices
    • Terminal strip numbering continuity checked from field to DCS/marshalling
    • Grounding and shielding scheme review against project grounding philosophy
    • Cable number cross-reference against cable and conduit schedule

  • Instrument Index Audit

    Tag-by-tag verification of instrument index entries against datasheets, P&IDs, and loop drawings. Missing tags, spec mismatches, and duplicate entries flagged.

    • Tag format and numbering convention compliance checked against project standards
    • Calibrated range vs. process range and turndown ratio verification
    • Spec class, line class, and flange rating cross-check per line list
    • Area classification consistency across index, P&IDs, and area classification drawings
    • Missing, duplicated, or orphaned tags flagged in comment register with P&ID reference

  • Electrical One-Line Review

    One-line diagram and panel schedule review against load calculations and applicable codes. Hazardous area classification verification included.

    • MCC, switchgear, and panel schedule load balance and ampacity verification
    • Overcurrent protection coordination review for motors, feeders, and branch circuits
    • Area classification and wiring method consistency per NEC Articles 501–503
    • Grounding electrode conductor sizing and equipment grounding continuity
    • E-stop, lockout, and safety interlock circuit tracing against cause-and-effect matrix

  • Comment Register

    Formal comment register with category, disposition, and response tracking. Delivered in your format — Word, Excel, or Bluebeam markup.

    • Comment register delivered in Excel, Word, or native Bluebeam markup — your format
    • Categories: Safety-Critical, Hold, Clarification, Minor — each with resolution path
    • Disposition tracking: accept, reject, or revise with response status per comment
    • Redline markup set keyed to comment numbers for direct drawing reference
    • Follow-up response round included for unresolved or revised items

Deliverable-Driven

OSHA Violations

E&I work carries real hazards — energized systems, classified areas, confined spaces, heavy equipment. We treat every task with the same level of planning and discipline, regardless of how routine it looks on paper. We do not accept pressure to skip steps when schedules are tight. A JSA takes ten minutes. An incident takes much longer to undo — if it can be undone at all.

Lockout / Tagout (LOTO)

  • Full written LOTO procedures before any work on energized systems
  • Individual locks — every technician carries their own, every time
  • Verification of zero-energy state before work begins
  • LOTO log maintained and signed off at task completion
  • Awareness training for techs working near energized equipment

Hot Work

  • Hot work permit required before any cutting, grinding, or welding
  • Continuous gas monitoring during all hot work in classified areas
  • Fire watch assigned and maintained for duration and cool-down period
  • Area inspected and signed off before work starts
  • Coordination with facility operator on permit issuance and isolation

Gas Detection

  • Personal 4-gas monitors required for all work in or near compression areas
  • Pre-entry bump test verified and logged at start of each shift
  • Calibration records maintained per manufacturer requirements
  • Clear alarm thresholds — work stops, area evacuated, no exceptions
  • Coordination with facility for classified-area entry

Fall Protection

  • Fall protection required at 4 feet or per site-specific threshold — whichever is lower
  • Harness inspection completed before each use
  • Tie-off points identified and verified before climbing begins
  • Aerial lift operators trained, checklist completed before each use
  • No improvised ladders or platforms; only rated equipment on the job

Tool & Equipment Safety

  • Insulated tools rated for electrical work on all E&I tasks
  • Hand tool inspection at start of each shift; damaged tools removed from service
  • Power tools GFCI-protected in wet or classified environments
  • No modified or field-fabricated tools permitted on any project
  • PPE matched to task — cut-resistant gloves, safety glasses, arc-rated clothing

Job Safety Analysis (JSA)

  • JSA completed before starting any new task or when conditions change
  • Developed by the crew doing the work — not imposed top-down
  • Each step reviewed for hazards; controls identified before work begins
  • All crew members sign the JSA and have the right to stop work
  • JSAs retained in project safety documentation file
Stop-Work Authority

Every Technician. Every Time.

Every Rogue technician has the authority — and the responsibility — to stop work if something doesn't look right. No explanation required, no pressure to push through. If the job can't be done safely, the job doesn't get done until it can. That's not a policy we wrote for a website. It's how our team has operated since day one.
